# Why Use Architectural Decision Records (ADRs)?

Architectural Decision Records (ADRs) are a crucial practice for software teams that want to build maintainable, scalable, and collaborative systems.

## What Are ADRs?

An ADR is a short document that captures an important architectural decision made along with its context and consequences. It's essentially a paper trail of your team's architectural thinking.

## Benefits of ADRs

### Knowledge Retention
- Captures the reasoning behind decisions made previously
- Prevents the loss of institutional knowledge and context
- Helps onboarding new team members quickly

### Communication
- Ensures that all team members understand key design choices
- Makes it easier to discuss and debate architectural approaches
- Helps with planning and future architectural work

### Decision Quality
- Forces teams to think through the implications of their choices
- Makes future decision-making more informed by historical context
- Enables better assessment of trade-offs and alternatives

### Collaboration
- Encourages team discussion around design choices
- Makes it easier to coordinate across teams
- Provides a shared reference point for discussions

## When to Use ADRs

- When making significant architectural or design decisions
- When choosing technology stacks or frameworks
- When implementing major system changes
- When resolving complex technical problems
- When there are multiple viable alternatives

## What Makes a Good ADR?

A good ADR should answer:
1. What was the context for the decision?
2. What was the decision made?
3. What were the alternatives considered?
4. Why was this specific choice made?
5. What are the consequences of this decision?

## Types of ADRs

### Proposed
A decision that is under consideration but not yet finalized

### Accepted
A decision that has been made and is implemented

### Deprecated
A decision that is no longer recommended but may still be in use

### Superseded by ADR-XXXX
A decision that has been replaced by a newer one

## ADRs in Practice

ADRs are most effective when they are:
- Created early in the decision process
- Updated as requirements change
- Made easily discoverable by team members
- Integrated into the team's workflow

The goal is to create a culture where documentation of decisions becomes as natural as making decisions themselves.
